Official policy quotes
"Preferred entertainment and media companies and eligible travelers are offered a special rate and generous bag allowance for cameras, film, lighting, sound equipment, musical instruments and other bags. Eligible travelers will pay $60* per piece, per one-way domestic trip, or $90 / £60 / €70, per one-way international trip. Fees for overweight and oversize bags will be waived.** Each eligible organization / company may travel with up to 25 pieces per flight, subject to seasonal and permanent bag embargoes. The maximum size and weight allowances are subject to the policies in place for the destination."
Source: aa.com — Special Items & Sports Equipment
"Media rates are effective for up to 25 checked pieces/bags total per flight for an organization or company (subject to operational limitations) and applies to: Audio equipment, Camera equipment, Lighting equipment, Photography equipment, Wardrobes/trunks, Musical Instruments. Bags may not exceed 100 lbs / 45 kg or 115 in / 292 cm in total dimensions (max length of 75 in / 190 cm)."
Source: saleslink.aa.com — Media Bags (updated October 2025)
"A media bag is a checked baggage related to the production of a: Movie, Television show, Photo shoot, Film festival, Print and/or news, Fashion or entertainment event."
Source: saleslink.aa.com — Media Bags
Required credentials at check-in
"Media bag rates apply to representatives of companies traveling with musical instruments, camera, film, video, lighting, or sound equipment. You must present your company issued identification (ID) or business card and your government issued picture ID for travel to receive the media bag rate."
Battery rules (AA-specific)
"Lithium-ion batteries removed from all equipment in their checked baggage and put into their carry-on bag. All batteries must be in good condition without any visible signs of damage. Batteries must have electrical tape over the contacts and placed in individual plastic bags. All batteries must be separated (i.e. no batteries may be attached to another battery). Up to 8 batteries of 100-watt hours (Wh) each may be accepted without prior approval authorization in their carry-on bag. Up to 2 batteries of 100–160Wh each may be accepted without prior approval authorization. Amounts above these quantities must have prior approval authorization — contact Reservations or American.Support@aa.com. We encourage customers to send their request 7 days prior to departure."
Source: saleslink.aa.com — Media Bags, Lithium-ion Batteries section

Alaska Airlines
alaskaair.com  ·  No dedicated media rate
No formal program Show media ID
Alaska Airlines does not have a dedicated media bag rate program. Show a media ID at check-in; standard excess bag fees apply, with a slight discount on 3rd+ bags. Bags 1–2 are standard rate ($25 each if under 50 lbs / 62 linear inches); bags 51–100 lbs or 63–115 linear inches cost $75 each; bags 3 and beyond are $50 each.
Policy excerpt
"Your 3rd and additional bags on Alaska will be $50 each instead of the usual $75. Your first and second bag on Alaska — that's under 50 lbs. and 62 linear inches — will cost you $25. Bags weighing 51–100 pounds or that are 63–115 inches will be $75 a pop. Just show an ID that indicates you're in the media when you get to the airport and you should be in the clear."
Source: dtxmedia.com — Airline Media Badge (Alaska Airlines section)

⚡ Universal battery rules (all airlines, FAA-mandated)

These apply regardless of airline — no exceptions
Spare lithium batteries must travel in carry-on only — never in checked gear. This includes all camera batteries, power banks, and spare laptop batteries.
Devices with batteries installed (cameras, laptops, phones) are allowed in both carry-on and checked baggage.
If your carry-on gets gate-checked, FAA rules require retrieval of any loose power banks before the aircraft door closes — mandatory bag retrieval, no exceptions.
American Airlines specifically requires batteries to have electrical tape over contacts and be in individual plastic bags, all separated from each other.
Sources: travelbaginsider.com  ·  saleslink.aa.com (AA battery rules)
Credential tip for solo operators: Media credentials can be a company-issued ID, company-named business card, or clearly labeled equipment cases with your company name/logo. Some agents will accept a business card alone; others require more. Best practice: bring a business card, have your LLC/company name on your cases, and be prepared to describe the production. For an AA pre-approval of higher battery quantities, email 7 days in advance.
